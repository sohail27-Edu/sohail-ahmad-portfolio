# Selected Direction — Warm Editorial Clarity

## Design Movement
Contemporary editorial minimalism with a tactile paper-and-ink sensibility, borrowing from independent magazines and modern identity systems rather than startup landing pages.

## Core Principles
1. **Clarity before decoration:** Every visual choice should improve scanning, confidence, or navigation.
2. **Warm restraint:** Use a small, purposeful palette and calm surfaces instead of dramatic gradients or effects.
3. **Human scale:** Pair precise structure with approachable copy, generous whitespace, and visible craft.
4. **Useful motion:** Transitions should clarify state and support wayfinding, never compete with the content.

## Color Philosophy
The visual system starts with a warm ivory background (#F5F1E8) to make the portfolio feel considered and human, then uses deep ink navy (#172A3A) for authority and legibility. A muted copper accent (#B86F4A) marks action and important moments without shouting. White keeps content areas open, while a near-black footer (#111820) provides a quiet close to the page. The palette should feel like paper, ink, and a single well-chosen editorial highlight.

## Layout Paradigm
Use a vertical narrative with asymmetric editorial moments: a compact sticky header, a hero with a left content column and right-side “working principles” panel, then alternating split layouts and offset card groupings. Avoid repetitive centered sections; let the page breathe with strong left edges, intentional line lengths, and occasional full-bleed color fields.

## Signature Elements
- A small copper rule/eyebrow system that repeats section labels and gives the page a recognizable editorial cadence.
- “SA” monogram mark rendered as a geometric stamp in the header and footer.
- Numbered process markers and oversized section numbers that make the work feel organized without adding clutter.

## Interaction Philosophy
Interactions should feel direct and obvious. Links expose a copper underline on hover, buttons shift slightly on press, the mobile menu opens as a calm sheet, and project cards respond with a modest lift and image scale. Focus rings remain visible and high contrast. No hidden gestures or decorative hover-only content.

## Animation
Use 180–260ms ease-out transitions for interactive states. On first load, hero copy may fade and rise a few pixels in sequence, while cards reveal with a short stagger only when they enter the viewport. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` by disabling nonessential transforms and entrance effects. Avoid looping motion, parallax, or layout-affecting animation.

## Typography System
Use **DM Serif Display** for the main hero name and major section headings to give the portfolio a crafted editorial voice. Use **Manrope** for navigation, body copy, labels, buttons, and metadata because it is clear at small sizes and pairs naturally with the serif display face. Headline hierarchy: 64–88px desktop hero, 44–56px section headings, 24–30px card titles. Body copy stays between 16–18px with generous line-height. Labels use uppercase 11–12px with 0.14em tracking.

## Brand Essence
A practical web developer for people who want a clean, reliable online presence without unnecessary complexity.

**Personality adjectives:** Clear, grounded, dependable.

## Brand Voice
Headlines are concise and confident. CTAs are direct and useful. Microcopy sounds like a capable collaborator: plainspoken, warm, and specific. Avoid inflated claims, buzzwords, and generic filler.

Example lines:
- “Good websites do less, better.”
- “Tell me what needs to work. I’ll help make it clear.”

## Wordmark & Logo
Use a custom geometric “SA” monogram: two interlocking vertical strokes with a diagonal cut that hints at a forward step. The mark should work as a compact stamp in a copper square, while the full name remains typeset separately in Manrope for legibility.

## Signature Brand Color
**Editorial Copper — #B86F4A.** It is warm, ownable, and restrained: distinct enough to identify Sohail’s work, but calm enough to preserve the portfolio’s practical tone.

## Style Decisions
- Use warm ivory, ink navy, white, and editorial copper as the core palette.
- Keep motion short, purposeful, and reduced-motion aware.
- Prefer asymmetric editorial structure over generic centered marketing layouts.
- Use DM Serif Display + Manrope instead of a default single sans-serif stack.
- Do not add testimonials, invented metrics, fake links, or unsupported credentials.

## Style Review Amendments
- Keep the SA mark visible as a geometric monogram inside a copper stamp in the header and footer, with a resilient text fallback.
- Use warm editorial mockups, paper surfaces, browser/product crops, or restrained navy/copper treatments for project visuals.
- Prefer specific, plainspoken benefits over broad claims such as “modern responsive websites.”
- Keep the skills and principles sections typographic and editorial rather than letting icons become the dominant personality signal.
